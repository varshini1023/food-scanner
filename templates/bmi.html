📊 Understanding BMI

Below 18.5 → Underweight
18.5 – 24.9 → Healthy weight
25.0 – 29.9 → Overweight
30.0 and above → Obesity

🥗 Make It Adaptable

🍽️ Build your plate

Instead of completely changing your diet, improve the balance of foods you already eat.

  • Add vegetables or greens to main meals.
  • Include a protein source such as dal, beans, pulses, paneer, milk or other suitable protein-rich foods.
  • Prefer whole grains and minimally processed foods more often.
  • Include different fruits and vegetables throughout the week.

💪 Don't ignore protein

Protein helps provide the building blocks needed for muscles and other body functions. It can come from both plant and animal sources.

🌾 Think about fiber

Pulses, vegetables, fruits and whole grains are useful sources of dietary fiber. WHO recommends adults and children over 10 aim for at least 25 g of naturally occurring dietary fiber per day.

🚶 Movement matters

You don't need to start with an extreme workout. Walking, cycling, household activity, sports and structured exercise can all contribute to physical activity. WHO recommends adults get at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity physical activity per week.

🧠 Reality Check

BMI is useful for screening, but it does not tell the complete story about your health.

BMI does not directly measure body fat and cannot distinguish between muscle, fat and bone. Your health picture also involves factors such as physical activity, diet, blood pressure, cholesterol, medical history and overall wellbeing.

So don't treat one BMI number as a judgment about your body. Use it as one piece of information and look at your habits and health over time.

Note: This calculator is intended for adults and provides general educational information. BMI is a screening measure and is not a diagnosis. If you have concerns about your weight or health, consult a qualified healthcare professional.
